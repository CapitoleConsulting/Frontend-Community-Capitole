# The current state of Frontend development at Capitole: challenges, opportunities and the birth of a needed community

Frontend development has evolved enormously in recent years. It's no longer just about "building screens"; it's about creating scalable, maintainable applications aligned with increasingly demanding quality standards.

In this context, tech companies and consultancies need more than good developers: they need a technical culture applied to every single developer.

At Capitole, there's a concrete reality that defines much of how frontend talent evolves within the company: many developers are seconded directly to the client.

---

## The advantage of working side-by-side with the end client

This model has a powerful advantage. Developers experience first-hand the technical reality of large companies, absorb their methodologies, workflows, architectures, and internal standards.

When the client has a mature and correct technical culture, this greatly accelerates professional growth. You learn to work with real good practices, maintainable code, testing, documentation, solid pipelines, and well-defined architectures.

In those cases, the experience gained is extremely valuable and the developer evolves surrounded by high-quality environments.

But here also appears the main problem and the "Achilles' heel" of many developers when they arrive.

---

## The risk of depending completely on the client's technical culture

A developer's experience cannot depend solely on the luck of landing in a technically sound project.

When the client doesn't care for the code, doesn't follow standards, ignores official documentation, or works with normalized bad practices, the developer ends up absorbing exactly that.

And, if there is no strong internal standard within the consultancy itself, the programmer has no reference to rely on.

That is probably one of the big current challenges of frontend within Capitole: the absence of a shared standard among developers.

There is no "Capitole way" to approach the frontend. There isn't yet a clear technical identity that accompanies the developer regardless of the project they end up working on.

And that has important consequences.

If Capitole had solid internal standards, shared documentation, common good practices, reference repositories, and a transversal technical culture, developers would arrive much better prepared at the client.

But beyond that, they could bring real technical value from day one.

They wouldn't just adapt to the client. They would also bring the Capitole quality with them.

---

## The birth of the Frontend Community

Precisely because of that need, the new Frontend Community of Capitole is born.

It's not the first attempt. In the past, something similar was tried, but the initiative failed to consolidate.

And it probably didn't fail due to lack of talent or technical interest, but because keeping a community alive requires much more than opening a Teams channel or creating a repository.

A technical community needs constant participation, role models, useful content, and, above all, motivation.

The big challenge is not creating the community; the real challenge is getting people to want to be part of it actively and sustainably over time.

---

## How to build a technical community that really works

Participation doesn't appear by itself. You have to generate it.

Internal talks, technical articles, workshops, and events can be one of the keys to activating interest.

Internal technical sessions help developers feel part of something bigger than their own client project.

It's also important to introduce more attractive dynamics:

- Internal hackathons
- Architecture challenges
- Awards and recognitions
- Spaces to share personal projects
- Lightning talks between colleagues

When a community manages to make learning entertaining and visible, participation changes completely.

Because in the end, developers don't just look for work. They also look to grow, share, and feel identified with a technical culture.

---

## Turning the community into the company's technical lung

A well-built frontend community can become one of the company's most important assets.

Especially for junior profiles.

Joining a large consultancy can be complicated when you land at a client from day one. Often, junior profiles feel lost, isolated, or without a clear reference on how to grow technically.

A strong community can solve much of that problem.

It can become:

- A support point
- A place to solve doubts
- A space to learn real standards
- A shared technical reference
- A safe environment to make mistakes and improve

And that not only improves developers individually. It also improves the overall quality the company can offer its clients.

Because a solid tech company is not built only by selling talent. It's also built by creating technical culture.

And that is probably where the future of frontend at Capitole really begins.
